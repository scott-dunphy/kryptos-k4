# "Mystified": what Sanborn expected solvers to get from his NYT material (Fable, 2026-10-18)

## 1. Source of the quote

- **It is not from any NYT article.** No NYT piece (2010 BERLIN, 2014 CLOCK, 2020 NORTHEAST), no Wired/Slate/NPR/SciAm coverage, and no 2025 auction/Kobek-Byrne coverage has Sanborn "mystified", "puzzled" or "baffled" by the lack of progress from the clues. The NYT itself cannot be fetched from here; its text was checked through the NBC syndication of the 2010 piece, the Cryptologic Foundation reprint of the 2014 piece, Klaus Schmeh's quotations of the 2020 piece, and kryptools.com's dated quote list.
- **The remark is second-hand dinner reportage, and it is about the K1/K2 coding charts, not about the K4 cribs.** Source: blog *Kryptos - Beyond K4* (kryptosfan.wordpress.com), post "Kryptos Notes From the ACA Convention", 28 Oct 2013, signed "Z", compiled from attendees' accounts of the dinner Q&A with Sanborn and Scheidt after the October 2013 American Cryptogram Association convention (Scheidt was a speaker). Copied verbatim on rumkin.com/reference/kryptos/k5/ (local copy data/web/rumkin_k5.html) and on the Yahoo-group mirror interred2.rssing.com (channel 1312950, page all_p3; not readable from here). Exact wording:
  > "Jim went on to say that he still believes there's something to be gained if we study his encryption charts that he released to the New York Times. He talked about how he released those in the context of we all wondered if the misspelled words were really mistakes, but he didn't elaborate past that. JS was also involved in some smaller group discussions where individuals said he brought up his K1/K2 matrix and seemed mystified no one has gotten any thing more from it."
  Same post: he recited the BERLIN crib "N = B, Y = E, P = R, V = L, T = I, T = N" and confirmed the one-to-one mapping "Emphatically"; asked whether K4 is a "one-off", Scheidt deferred to him and he said "we don't answer it"; the buried object was "a bronze USGS marker".
- Reliability: third-hand ("individuals said he ... seemed mystified"); the direct part ("something to be gained if we study his encryption charts") is a paraphrase of what he said to the table. The "encryption charts that he released to the New York Times" are the K1 and K2 coding sheets published with the 2010 article (kryptools: one PDF created 20 Nov 2010; K1 full, K2 lines 1-6 of 12 only) plus the 2010 K4 "clue sheet" (PDF created 17 Nov 2010; NYPVTT written above BERLIN with the '?' included).
- Nearest first-hand statements of surprise: NYT 21 Nov 2010, Sanborn: "I assumed the code would be cracked in a fairly short time"; Wired/rumkin interview: "I had figured that the parts that have been solved already would have been done a lot quicker than they [were]"; NYT 21 Nov 2014: "I figured maybe I should be a little more specific" (reason for the CLOCK clue). None says the cribs should have sufficed.

## 3. Why he might expect the material to be enough: hypotheses, predictions, status

Read with the correct referent (the K1/K2 charts), the question is "what more is in the charts?" The crib-based readings are kept because that is how the user framed it and because the 2014 clue was explicitly meant to be "more specific".

## 4. Diagnostic run today (scratchpad `nyt_weakness_diag.py`, 20k shuffles, seconds)

- Self-encryptions among the 24 cribs: real 2 (S->S@32, K->K@73). Null shuffled CT P(>=2) = 0.273; null random English-frequency cribs on the real CT P(>=2) = 0.188.
- Equal-CT cross-crib pairs: real 6. Shuffled CT P(>=6) = 0.419.
- KA-Vigenère implied key: letters equal to the tableau's zero letter (K) real 2, P(>=2) = 0.273; longest common substring of the two 13/11-letter key fragments real 1, P(>=1) = 0.994. Same for AZ-Vig, Beaufort, variant Beaufort (lcs 1 in all six).
- Planted check: KA-Vig with key row PALIMPSEST on a text carrying the cribs gives implied fragments ALIMPSESTPALI | IMPSESTPALI, lcs 11, keyword readable. So a K1/K2-style key row would have been visible from the cribs alone; K4's is not.
- Conclusion: none of the "visible weaknesses" a reader could spot in the NYT cribs is statistically notable, and the K1/K2 mechanism leaves no fingerprint in the crib keystream.

## 5. Ranking and what (not) to do

1. **H3 + H8 (he knows the chart, so the cribs look sufficient to him; he does not model the search).** Highest prior, consistent with every negative, no test needed. The "mystified" remark concerns the K1/K2 charts, whose only "more" is the hand-alteration habit (key-row C-for-S slip, post-chart copper change, misspelled plaintext) = the mask/nuance theory already adopted; it is not decodable from 24 cribs (`run_chartdecode.py`, `run_fingerprint_decode.py`: real = null).
2. **H1 (same sheet mechanics with a non-keyword key row).** The keyword version is dead with up to 3 alterations; a hand-drawn numeric row is exactly the untestable aperiodic nuance key. Only K5 depth (`k5_depth.py`, column mode) can decide it.
3. **H4 (clock as method hint).** Thoroughly covered; the 2025 Weltzeituhr identification plus "Who says it is even a math solution?" shift it to theme. Remaining untestable corner: free-alphabet 24-face Quagmire III.

No cheap crib-algebra test remains open from this question. Two specs for the record, both low value:
