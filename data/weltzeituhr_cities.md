# Urania-Weltzeituhr (Alexanderplatz, Berlin) - city names per face

Compiled 2026-10-17 from web sources. Only what a source shows is recorded. Confidence flags at the bottom.

## Sources
- [HU] Hungarian Wikipedia "Uránia világóra", section "Az órán szereplő városok" (wikitext, curid=1498358): https://hu.wikipedia.org/?curid=1498358  -- 24 lines, one per face, in ring order; German spellings as engraved. Cites Helmut Caspar, Märkische Allgemeine 1997-12-24 ("Ärger mit der Weltzeituhr am Alex. Städtenamen sind nicht korrekt"). Total 147 entries = 146 places + "Datumsgrenze". POST-1997 layout.
- [OFF] Official clock site, table "Liste der Städte auf der Weltzeituhr": https://www.weltzeituhr-berlin.de/en/places-worldtimeclock  -- 146 places with real-world UTC offset (sorted by offset, NOT by engraved face; corrected modern names e.g. "Bratislava (Pressburg)", "Neu-Delhi", "Ulaanbaatar"). Site text: "146 names of cities and regions ... along with the date line" and "original 80 city names from all 24 time zones" (1969): https://www.weltzeituhr-berlin.de/en/urania-worldtimeclock
- [DEWP] de.wikipedia "Weltzeituhr (Alexanderplatz)" wikitext: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weltzeituhr_(Alexanderplatz) -- 1997 changes: Leningrad -> Sankt Petersburg, Alma Ata -> Almaty, Bratislava -> Pressburg (despite Slovak protest, Berliner Zeitung 1997-12-20 https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/15998478), 20 cities added incl. Jerusalem, Tel Aviv; zone assignment changed (Kiew).
- [ENWP] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Clock_(Alexanderplatz) -- 20 cities omitted by GDR added, e.g. Tel Aviv, Cape Town, Seoul; Kyiv moved from Moscow time to EET.
- [BZ] Berliner Zeitung 1997-12-12 "Frisch poliert: Die Weltzeituhr dreht sich bald": https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/20-staedtenamen-wurden-bei-der-restaurierung-ergaenzt-frisch-poliert-die-weltzeituhr-dreht-sich-bald-li.7079 -- Oslo had been "schlicht vergessen"; Jerusalem, Kapstadt deliberately omitted by GDR; Kiew formerly on Moscow time.

## Pre-1997 (1969-1997) layout: NOT FOUND as a list
No source located that lists the original names per face. What is documented:
- Original had ~80 names per [OFF] ("original 80 city names from all 24 time zones"), not ~126 (that figure was in the task brief; no source found).
- Names changed in 1997: Leningrad (now Sankt Petersburg, face 3), Alma Ata (now Almaty, face 6), Bratislava (now Pressburg, face 1) [DEWP].
- 20 names added in 1997 [DEWP/ENWP/BZ]: confirmed members: Oslo, Jerusalem, Tel Aviv, Kapstadt, Seoul. The other 15 not identified in any source I could read.
- Kiew reassigned from the Moscow-time face (face 3) to the EET face (face 2) [BZ/DEWP].
- Also 2015 restoration is reported by travel sites (no detail on names).
- Likely sources to pursue: the Berliner Zeitung 1997 articles (full text), Märkische Allgemeine 1997-12-24 (Caspar), taz 1997-12-20 https://taz.de/!1367509/, DDR-era photos (Bundesarchiv Bild 183-* on Commons category Urania-Weltzeituhr), DDR Urania-Weltzeituhr brochures, Erich John interviews. Commons has no per-face close-up files that I could find (category list checked: only general views).

Sanborn said (2014) "there are several really interesting clocks in Berlin"; secondary reports (search summary of solvekryptos.com, 2025) state he confirmed the referent is the Weltzeituhr. Treat as secondary/unverified.

## Confidence
- Post-1997 face grouping and order: medium-high. Single transcription [HU] (one Wikipedia source, but its 147 entries exactly match [OFF]'s 146 + date line, and spellings are German). Within-face order trusted as shown but unverified against the physical clock. A trailing comma after "Dhaka" in the +6 line may hide nothing (count still matches).
- Face 9 holds both +9 and +10 cities and face 10 is +11 by HU grouping; this is how HU transcribes it; verify against photos.
- Pre-1997: gap, only deltas.
